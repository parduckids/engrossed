Fossick: Terms of Use

For the app on iPhone. Last updated 31 October 2026

The short version. Fossick is a notebook and a guide, not the source of truth. Rules, seasons, maps, depths, tides, your trail and the way back can all be incomplete, out of date or simply wrong. Always check the official sources and the signs on the ground, and be ready to find your own way. You are responsible for what you do outdoors.

Rules and limits

What Fossick shows about public land, fishing size and bag limits, marine parks and sanctuary zones is a summary made from public government information, simplified, and checked for updates about once a day. Rules change, places have local exceptions, closures happen at short notice, and our summary or the boundaries we draw can be wrong. The managing authority, its current published rules and the signs on site always win. Check them before you collect, fish, dig, camp or bring a dog, and get permission before going onto private land. Any fine or consequence for breaking a rule is yours, even if Fossick showed something different.

Food and foraging

Fossick never tells you that anything is safe to eat or to touch. Seasons and categories in the app are a record of your own finds and general timing, not identification. Never eat a mushroom, plant, shellfish or anything else unless you are certain what it is from a reliable expert source, and check local health warnings for shellfish and water quality.

Maps, location and the way back

The maps, contour lines, public land boundaries and labels come from open data and may be inaccurate or missing. GPS on a phone can be off by tens of metres or more under trees, in gullies, near cliffs or in poor weather, and can stop when the battery runs low. Your recorded trail, the arrow and distance back, the time to turn back before sunset and the offer to finish a walk are estimates from that GPS. A straight line back can cross cliffs, water or thick scrub; your recorded trail can too. Do not rely on Fossick alone to find your way: carry a map and compass or a dedicated GPS, a charged battery, water and suitable gear, tell someone where you are going, and consider a personal locator beacon in remote country.

The sea

Depth shading and depth lines are approximate, drawn from coarse global data, and are not for navigation. Swell, waves, water temperature and tides come from a free forecast service and are forecasts, which can be wrong, especially close to shore. Never use Fossick to decide whether it is safe to go on the water, onto rock platforms or across a bar. Use official marine forecasts, tide tables and nautical charts.

Emergencies

In an emergency in Australia call 000 (or 112 from a mobile). The emergency screen in Fossick only shows your position to read out; it does not call for help by itself and may be wrong if the GPS is.
